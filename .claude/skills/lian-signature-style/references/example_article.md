abstract: |
  This review assesses Agbola and Acupan's (2010) study of the push factors behind emigration from the Philippines. The authors estimate an error correction model on yearly national data for 1975–2005 and find that unemployment raises emigration, while adult literacy, political instability and population density lower it. The methods are applied carefully, but the sample is small (31 observations for ten explanatory terms), two proxies are weak, and pull factors in destination countries are left out. We suggest four extensions, the most important being separate models for temporary and permanent migration.

# Introduction

About 8.2 million Filipinos live or work in more than 193 countries, and they sent home US\$14.4 billion in remittances in 2007 [@agbola2010]. Sending workers abroad began in the 1970s as a short-term fix for unemployment. It has since become a permanent part of the economy. Yet, as the authors note, the causes of emigration from the Philippines are "not well established."

The study asks one question: how do economic, demographic and political factors at home affect emigration? A *push factor* is a condition at home that pushes people out, such as high unemployment. A *pull factor* is a condition abroad that draws them in, such as higher wages.

# Theoretical framework

The authors combine four theories. In the classic models, people migrate when they expect a better life abroad [@ravenstein1885; @todaro1969]. Human capital theory treats migration as an investment, so more educated people should migrate more. The Roy model, as applied by @borjas1987, says that income inequality decides which skill groups leave.

The resulting model, in words, is

$$ m_t = f(c_t,\ \ell_t,\ g_t,\ g_t^2,\ s_t,\ \pi_t,\ d_t,\ u_t,\ y_t) \qquad\qquad (1) $$

where $m_t$ is the migration share in year $t$, $c_t$ the cost of migration, $\ell_t$ adult literacy, $g_t$ income inequality, $s_t$ political instability, $\pi_t$ inflation, $d_t$ population density, $u_t$ unemployment and $y_t$ per capita income.

The positive unemployment coefficient fits the idea of emigration as a "safety valve" when there are not enough jobs at home. The negative literacy coefficient is the opposite of what human capital theory predicts.

# Critical assessment

The study's strengths are an important question and careful time-series steps: unit root tests, cointegration tests, a general-to-specific model and diagnostic tests that all pass. Its weaknesses are three. First, the sample is small: 31 yearly observations for ten explanatory terms. Second, airfare stands in for the full cost of migration, and adult literacy stands in for education.[^proxy] Third, only push factors are modelled.

[^proxy]: A *proxy* is a stand-in measure for something that cannot be measured directly.
